Experimental work of a Master's thesis that tries to predict how a loudspeaker is perceived from objective measurements alone. Fifteen listeners rated four woofers and four tweeters on loudness, timbral balance and preference in a controlled listening room, with switching between the speakers driven in Reaper over OSC and a test interface written in MATLAB. In parallel, thirteen spectral descriptors (including centroid, rolloff, kurtosis and entropy) are extracted from the anechoic frequency response and the distortion measurements of each driver.

Ratings are aggregated with a STATIS-based correlation weighting that reduces the weight of inconsistent raters. For each attribute, linear regression, stepwise regression and polynomial support vector regression are compared, with ReliefF feature selection and evaluation on RMSE and R². One-way and two-way ANOVA, in MATLAB and in Python, checks whether the perceived differences are significant. The main part of the work, namely the test interface, measurement handling and statistics, is in MATLAB.
